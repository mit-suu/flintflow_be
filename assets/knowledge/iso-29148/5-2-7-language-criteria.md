topic: requirements
description: Wording rules for requirements - binding verbs and vague terms to avoid
status: verified

ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 gives wording rules so that requirements stay precise:

- Use **"shall"** for a binding requirement, **"should"** for a goal or preference, **"may"** for an option, and
  **"will"** for a statement of fact or of what an external party does. "Must" is best avoided because it is easily
  confused with "shall".
- State requirements in the **positive** ("the system shall ..."), not as what the system shall not do, unless a
  negative is really needed.
- Prefer the **active voice**, so the subject that performs the action is named.
- Avoid **vague and unverifiable terms**, for example superlatives ("best", "most"), subjective words
  ("user-friendly", "easy", "fast"), vague pronouns ("it", "this" without a clear referent), comparative phrases
  ("better than", "higher quality"), loopholes ("if possible", "as appropriate", "as applicable"), open-ended lists
  ("including but not limited to", "etc.") and absolutes that cannot be proven ("always", "never", "100 percent").

When a vague word is found, replace it with a measurable value, for example "fast" becomes "within 2 seconds for 95
percent of requests".
